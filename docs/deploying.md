# Deploying

The node goes outside in a box you drill and seal yourself. Two ports in the
lid, one vent, one gland for the antenna, one for the panel cable. About an
hour, then an overnight cure.

## Why the box needs ports

Two reasons a camera cannot just sit in a clear box:

- The motion sensor senses heat, and plastic or glass blocks it. It needs an
  open port with its dome poking through.
- A window sitting a centimetre in front of the lens fogs and hazes every
  shot. The lens needs its own small window, right up against it.

## 1. Mark the lid

All the ports go in the hinged lid, and the board mounts to the inside of
the lid. That keeps the lens against its window and the camera ribbon short
and still.

1. Hold the board against the inside of the lid where it will live.
2. Mark the lens centre and the motion sensor centre straight off the
   board.
3. Mark a spot for the vent plug, away from the other two.

## 2. Drill

Clamp the lid to a scrap board first. Polycarbonate blows out a crater on
the back side of a hole with nothing behind it.

1. Lens port: about **13 mm**, just wide enough for the camera's field of
   view.
2. Motion sensor port: about **10.5 mm**, so the dome pokes through.
3. Vent hole: sized to your vent plug.

Low speed on the drill. High speed melts polycarbonate instead of cutting
it and welds the chip back into the hole. Back the bit out to clear chips.
Deburr every hole on both faces.

## 3. The lens window

1. Run a thin bead of silicone around the **rim only** of the acrylic disc.
2. Set it on the **inside** face of the lid over the lens port, so the lid
   shields the joint from weather.
3. Slip the foam ring over the camera's lens barrel. It is a light seal
   between the lens and the window.

!!! warning "Three things that ruin windows"
    Use **neutral-cure** silicone, the "window and door" kind. The
    vinegar-smelling kind (acetoxy) cracks polycarbonate over time. Never
    wipe the acrylic disc with alcohol or ammonia glass cleaner; it crazes
    into a fog. No superglue anywhere near the window; the fumes haze it
    permanently.

## 4. The motion sensor

The sensor is glued in, not screwed.

1. Push the white dome through its port from the inside, flush or a hair
   proud.
2. Run a bead of silicone around the **base** of the dome to hold and seal
   it.
3. Keep the silicone off the white cap. The cap is what the sensor sees
   through.

## 5. Mount the board

1. Bond nylon standoffs to the inside of the lid with adhesive. Do not
   drill through the weather face to mount the board.
2. Screw the board to the standoffs with the lens sitting a millimetre or
   two behind its window.
3. Fit the antenna bulkhead pigtail through a gland or a drilled hole in
   the side of the box, nut on the inside, antenna on the outside.
4. Bring the panel cable in through a gland.

Only round cables cross the hinge between lid and base. The flat camera
ribbon never does; it cracks from the flexing. Leave a service loop so the
lid opens fully.

## 6. Close it up

1. Drop in the vent plug and a desiccant pack.
2. Seat the lid gasket and close the lid.
3. Let the silicone cure for a full **24 hours** before the node goes
   outside.

The box is **not** supposed to be airtight. The vent lets it breathe so it
does not fog from the inside; the gasket and the glands keep the rain out.

## 7. Hang it

1. Mount the node chest high on a tree, facing where the animals move.
2. Tilt the lens slightly down and give it a small hood or brim, so rain
   sheds off the window instead of sitting on it.
3. Point the panel at the open sky, south if you can.

A node needs a radio path to a gateway or another node. Trees and hills
cost range; height buys it. If the node drops out of the app after
hanging, move it, or add a relay between it and the gateway.
